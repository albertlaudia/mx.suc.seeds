Vitamin D3 buying guide 2026: which form, which dose, which brand

2026-09-22 · 6 min read · Guide · by Sindo Wellness

Vitamin D3 is one of the most-searched supplements worldwide, especially in tropical regions where sun exposure is high but indoor lifestyle is dominant. This guide covers the three forms (cholecalciferol vs calcitriol vs ergocalciferol), the doses backed by research, the brands we trust, and how to pair D3 with K2 for better absorption.

The three forms

Cholecalciferol (D3) is the form your skin produces from sunlight. It is the form most studied for supplementation and the form we recommend. Look for it on the label as "cholecalciferol."

Calcitriol (active D3) is the metabolically active form. It is a prescription medication in most countries and is rarely sold as a supplement. Skip it unless prescribed.

Ergocalciferol (D2) is plant-derived. It is cheaper but raises 25(OH)D less efficiently than D3. Skip it for daily supplementation.

Which dose

For most adults without a diagnosed deficiency, 1,000 – 2,000 IU (25 – 50 mcg) per day is the maintenance dose backed by the Endocrine Society and the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements.

Higher doses (4,000 IU / 100 mcg) may be appropriate short-term under medical supervision if you have a documented deficiency. Do not self-prescribe high doses — vitamin D is fat-soluble and accumulates, so toxicity is possible (though rare).

Pair D3 with K2

D3 increases calcium absorption. K2 (specifically the MK-7 form) directs that calcium into bones rather than soft tissue. Many users report better results when taking both together. Look for a D3 + K2 combo or buy them separately.

When to take it

D3 is fat-soluble, so it absorbs best with a meal that contains fat. Many users prefer breakfast or lunch; taking it at night can occasionally disrupt sleep in sensitive individuals.
